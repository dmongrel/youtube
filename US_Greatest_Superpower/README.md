# US Greatest Superpower: sources

Sources for the claims in the video, in chapter order. Times match the published video. Each note sums up what its sources support, and where a source can't settle a point, the note says so.

Where the video uses an estimate or our own comparison, it says "reportedly", "around" or "estimated", and the notes below explain why.

## 0:27 Intro

- **Carriers:** the US Navy has 11 aircraft carriers in 2026, ten Nimitz-class plus USS Gerald R. Ford. Nimitz's retirement has been pushed back to March 2027, so the count may change after that.
- **Military aircraft:** the US has about 13,000 military aircraft (FlightGlobal, World Air Forces 2026).
- **Spending:** SIPRI's 2025 figures, published April 2026, put US military spending at $954bn. That is more than the next six countries combined ($905bn). A year earlier it was more than the next nine, which is why the video says "the next several".

## 1:41 Eyes In Orbit

- **CORONA:** it began in the late 1950s as a joint CIA–Air Force programme.
  - The first success was Discoverer XIV on 18 August 1960. A USAF C-119J caught its film capsule in mid-air at 8,000 ft the next day.
  - There were 145 launches, the last on 25 May 1972. The programme was declassified in 1995.
- **The NRO:** the National Reconnaissance Office was created in September 1961 and took over CORONA. The government acknowledged it existed on 18 September 1992.
- **The 2019 launch-pad photo:** on 30 August 2019 the president tweeted a photo of Iran's Semnan Launch Site One. It came from an intelligence briefing and was not an official release.
  - Satellite tracker Marco Langbroek matched it to the KH-11 satellite USA 224.
  - Published resolution estimates run from "just under 10 cm" to "well below 20 cm".
  - The dinner-plate comparison is ours, not a source's.
- **The telescopes:** in June 2012 the NRO donated two unused 2.4 m telescopes to NASA. Hubble's mirror is also 2.4 m, and one of the two became the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.

## 4:06 Crypto AG

These points come from the Washington Post / ZDF investigation of February 2020 ("Operation Rubicon").

- **Ownership:**
  - The CIA and West Germany's BND secretly bought Crypto AG through shell companies in 1970.
  - The CIA bought out the BND's share for $17M in 1993.
  - The CIA owned the company until it was liquidated in 2018, 48 years in all.
- **Before the purchase:** the NSA had a "gentlemen's understanding" with the founder, Boris Hagelin, from the early 1950s.
- **Customers:** about 120 countries bought its machines; the National Security Archive says "over 100". They included Iran, Latin American governments, India and Pakistan.
- **Profits and verdict:** the CIA and BND split the profits. The CIA's own history calls it "the intelligence coup of the century".

## 4:47 Cables

- **Operation Ivy Bells** ran from 1971 to 1981.
  - The tapped cable lay nearly 400 ft down, inside Soviet territorial waters.
  - A 20-ft pod recorded the traffic without piercing the cable, and divers collected the tapes every month.
  - Much of the traffic was unencrypted.
- **The cable's route:** secondary sources say it linked the Petropavlovsk submarine base to the mainland. The video only says "one of its submarine bases".
- **Ronald Pelton** worked at the NSA from 1965 to 1979, then resigned.
  - As a former analyst, he walked into the Soviet embassy in January 1980.
  - He sold secrets from 1980 to 1985 for about $35,000.
  - He was arrested in 1985 and given three life sentences in 1986.

## 5:28 Steal

**U-505.**
- **The capture:** depth charges from USS Chatelain forced U-505 to the surface on 4 June 1944, about 150 miles off Rio de Oro.
  - The ship was part of Task Group 22.3 under Capt. Daniel V. Gallery, built around USS Guadalcanal.
  - The crew had partly scuttled the boat, and US sailors boarded it while it was sinking.
- **The haul:** two Enigma machines and the codebooks.
- **Afterwards:**
  - The U-boat was towed 1,700 miles to Bermuda, arriving 19 June, and kept secret.
  - It has been at Chicago's Museum of Science and Industry since 1954.
  - It was the first enemy warship the US Navy had boarded and captured on the high seas since 1815.

- <https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-064/h-064-3.html>
- <https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/world-war-ii/1944/u-505-capture.html>
- <https://www.msichicago.org/explore/whats-here/exhibits/u-505-submarine/story/the-first-exhibition/the-trip-to-chicago>

**Haigerloch.**
- **The reactor:** the German B-VIII experimental reactor sat in a rock cellar under Haigerloch's Schlosskirche. It never reached criticality.
- **The race:** French troops reached the town on 22 April 1945 but didn't find the lab. The US Alsos mission found it the next day. The race was against the French occupation zone, not the Soviets.
- **The haul:**
  - 659 of the 664 uranium cubes (2.4 kg each, about 1.5 tonnes in all) were dug up from a field beside the church.
  - The heavy water was taken.
  - The scientists were picked up at Hechingen and Tailfingen, and Heisenberg at Urfeld on 2 May. They later went to Farm Hall.

- <https://physicstoday.aip.org/letters/haigerloch-cave-survived-the-war-1760119213642>
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haigerloch_atomic_pile>
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsos_Mission>

**Special Mission V-2.**
- **The shipment:** 341 railcars left Nordhausen between 22 and 31 May 1945.
  - They went via Antwerp and a Liberty ship to New Orleans, then by rail to White Sands.
  - They carried parts for about 100 V-2s, not 100 finished rockets.
- **The deadline:** the Soviet takeover of the area was scheduled for 1 July and happened around mid-July.

- <https://www.defensemedianetwork.com/stories/special-mission-v-2/3/>

**The Lunik.** From "The Kidnapping of the Lunik" by Sydney Wesley Finer (CIA, Studies in Intelligence 11/1, 1967).
- **The spacecraft:** a real production craft, though its engine and most of its electronics had been removed.
- **The borrowing:**
  - The whole truck was diverted to a rented salvage yard.
  - The team worked from 7:30 pm to 4:00 am, and the crate was back for delivery at 7:00 am.
  - One Soviet seal was cut and replaced with a duplicate.
- **The outcome:** there was "no indication the Soviets ever discovered" it.
- **Date and place:** the CIA text gives neither. Around 1959–60, in Mexico, is Brugioni's account.

**USS Halibut.**
- **The recovery:** in October 1971, in the Sea of Okhotsk, Halibut recovered Soviet test-missile debris: "more than two million pieces", none bigger than 6 inches. It was reassembled at the Naval Research Laboratory.
- **The cover story:** the mission also served as cover for the Ivy Bells tap.
- **The source:** this account traces to Blind Man's Bluff (pp. 188–189). The book names the missile as the SS-N-12, but that isn't independently confirmed, so the video doesn't name it.

**The Pantsir.**
- **The capture:** a Russian-made Pantsir-S1, supplied by the UAE, was captured at al-Watiya airbase in Libya in May 2020.
- **The flight:** The Times reported in January 2021 that a USAF C-17 flew it from Zuwarah to Ramstein in June 2020.
- **Official response:** the Pentagon did not comment.

## 7:56 Finding Out

**The capture.**
- **When and where:** Saddam Hussein was captured at about 8 pm on 13 December 2003, at a farm near ad-Dawr (Operation Red Dawn).
- **Who:** the 4th Infantry Division and Task Force 121.
- **How he was found:** link analysis of his bodyguards (the al-Muslit brothers), drivers and five families.
  - The final tip came from Mohammed Ibrahim al-Muslit, captured on 12–13 December.
  - Task Force 121 had run 12 failed raids for Saddam between July and December 2003.

- <https://www.army.mil/article/116559/operation_red_dawn_nets_saddam_hussein>
- <https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2003/12/mil-031214-afps03.htm>
- <https://www.cnn.com/2003/WORLD/meast/12/14/sprj.irq.saddam.operation/>

**The hideout.**
- **Gen. Sanchez's briefing (14 December 2003):**
  - "a small walled compound with a metal lean-to structure, a mud hut";
  - a hole "six to eight feet deep", with room to lie down, "camouflaged with brick and dirt";
  - recovered: two AK-47s, a pistol, "$750,000 U.S. dollars in 100-dollar denominations" and a taxi;
  - "captured without resistance".
- **Maj. Gen. Odierno:**
  - "two small rooms in an adobe hut", "one chair, one bed and lots of clothes strewn all over", and a kitchen with running water;
  - the hole was closed with "a Styrofoam insert and a rug... covered with dirt";
  - Saddam "had a pistol on him, which he never used".
- **The AP's tour** found a fridge with Bounty bars, hot dogs and a can of 7-Up, dirty dishes, and a ventilation pipe.
- **The hut and the hole:** he lived in the hut; the hole was only the hiding place.
- **The quote:** US officers relayed his words as "I am Saddam Hussein, I am the president of Iraq and I am willing to negotiate."

## 10:25 Underground

- **Fordow:** the site is about 80–90 m under a mountain, roughly 27–30 stories at about 3 m a storey.
- **The bomb:** the GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator weighs about 30,000 lb. The B-2 is the only aircraft in service that carries it.
- **Operation Midnight Hammer (June 2025):**
  - Seven B-2s flew from Whiteman AFB, Missouri, a round trip of about 37 hours without landing.
  - Decoy B-2s flew west over the Pacific.
  - In all, 14 MOPs fell on Fordow and Natanz.
- **How the bombs went in:** Gen. Caine's briefing on 26 June described six bombs for each Fordow ventilation shaft.
  - The first removed the concrete cap.
  - Bombs 2–5 went down the shaft at over 1,000 ft/s to "explode in the mission space".
  - The sixth was a spare that could go where needed.
  - Two MOPs hit Natanz.
- **Tolkien:** the dwarves "delved too greedily and too deep".

## 11:16 Cyber

- **Natanz:** the halls are about 8 m below ground under concrete and earth, and the network had no internet connection.
- **How Stuxnet got in:**
  - Early versions spread only by USB stick (Zetter), probably through contractors.
  - Sanger says the exact route "remains secret".
  - The worm was discovered in June 2010.
- **What it did:**
  - It pushed the centrifuges' frequency up to 1,410 Hz, then down toward 2 Hz.
  - Meanwhile it replayed 21 seconds of recorded normal sensor readings to the operators (Langner).
  - The Institute for Science and International Security (ISIS) estimates that about 1,000 IR-1 centrifuges were "decommissioned and replaced".
- **Who:** the operation was called "Olympic Games" (NYT, 1 June 2012). US–Israeli responsibility has never been officially acknowledged.
- **Ocean's Eleven (2001):** the crew feeds Benedict footage shot in a replica vault.

## 12:08 Deep Sea

- **K-129:** a Soviet diesel submarine carrying three SS-N-4 nuclear ballistic missiles. It sank in March 1968.
- **Finding it:** "After the Soviets abandoned their extensive search efforts, the US located the submarine" (CIA Museum). It lay 16,500 ft down, "more than three miles".
- **The recovery:**
  - The ship was the Hughes Glomar Explorer, with the cover story that it was "mining manganese nodules".
  - Its claw gripped a 1,750-ton, 132-ft section of the hull. About 90% broke away and fell back, leaving a 38-ft forward section (declassified CIA history).
  - Six Soviet submariners recovered with it were buried at sea with honours.
- **The "Glomar response"** ("we can neither confirm nor deny") comes from the CIA's reply to a FOIA request by Rolling Stone reporter Harriet Ann Phillippi (Phillippi v. CIA, D.C. Cir. 1976).
